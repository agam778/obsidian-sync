## Soldering
Soldering uses a filler metal that melts below $450^\circ\text{C}$ while the base material remains solid. It is especially important for electrical, electronic connections, wires, conductors, and low temperature joining.

### Basic Sequence
```text
Clean ➔ Apply Flux (if required) ➔ Heat Joint ➔ Apply Solder ➔ Solder Melts & Wets Surfaces ➔ Remove Heat ➔ Solidification ➔ Inspect
```

## Solder Alloys

### Traditional Alloys
* **Tin-Lead Based ($Sn\text{--}Pb$):** Traditional alloy, no longer favored / phased out due to environmental and health issues.
### Lead-Free Solder Alloys ($SAC$)
* **SAC** stands for **$Sn\text{--}Ag\text{--}Cu$** (Tin-Silver-Copper):
  * **SAC 305:** $96.5\%\text{ }Sn,\ 3\%\text{ }Ag,\ 0.5\%\text{ }Cu$
  * **SAC 105:** $98.5\%\text{ }Sn,\ 1\%\text{ }Ag,\ 0.5\%\text{ }Cu$
  * **SAC 405:** $95.5\%\text{ }Sn,\ 4\%\text{ }Ag,\ 0.5\%\text{ }Cu$
## Soldering Processes

* **Hand Soldering:** Manual soldering with a soldering iron.
* **Wave Soldering:** PCB passes over a wave of molten solder.
* **Reflow Soldering:** Solder paste printing followed by controlled heating.
* **Selective Soldering:** Only selected PCB areas are soldered.
* **Laser Soldering:** Localized heating for precise automated joining.
## Common Soldering Defects

- Cold solder joint
- Solder bridge
- Insufficient solder
- Excess solder
- Voids
- Non-wetting

# Brazing

Brazing is a joining process in which the joining temperature is between **$450^\circ\text{C}$ to $1200^\circ\text{C}$**. It is one of the oldest processes used for joining of copper, bronze, and iron, developed with improving metallurgy.

### Brazing Sequence
```text
Clean Surface ➔ Prepare Joint ➔ Apply Flux (if needed) ➔ Assemble with Correct Clearance ➔ Heat ➔ Melt Filler ➔ Filler Flows by Capillary Action ➔ Cool ➔ Remove Flux ➔ Inspect
```

---

## Brazing Process Mechanisms

* **Wetting:** Spreading of molten filler metal over the surface (kisi cheez ko geela karna).
* **Capillary Action:** Molten metal must flow into the gap / clearance between closely fitted surfaces.
* **Diffusion:** Metallurgical reaction between the filler and base metal.
* **Flux:** Applied to protect the joint from oxidation and ensure clean bonding surfaces.

---

## Brazing Filler Metals

1. **Silver-Based:** Mainly for high ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC).
2. **Copper-Phosphorus:** Widely used for copper joining without external flux.
3. **Brass ($Cu\text{--}Zn$):** General structural and engineering brazing.
4. **Nickel-Based:** Important for high temperature applications, stainless steel, and aerospace components.
5. **Aluminium-Based:** Suitable for aluminium ($Al$) based alloys.

# Comparison: Soldering vs. Brazing vs. Welding

| Features | Soldering | Brazing | Welding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base metal melts?** | No | No | Yes (Usually) |
| **Filler melts?** | Yes | Yes | Sometimes |
| **Temperature** | $< 450^\circ\text{C}$ | $> 450^\circ\text{C}$ | Higher |
| **Heat Input** | Low | Low to Moderate | Often High |
| **Dissimilar metals** | Good | Excellent | Difficult |
